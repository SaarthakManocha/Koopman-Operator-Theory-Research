Where these numbers come from

Kernel tracepoints sched_switch, sched_wakeup and sched_migrate_task, recorded live. Every reconstructed process was checked against the kernel's own counters in /proc/<pid>/schedstat: CPU time within about 1%, queue waiting time within about 2%.

How to read the machine

A tile is a core; it glows in the colour of the process it is running, and violet when it runs a kernel or system task. Tokens under a tile are waiting in that core's run queue, with a bar that grows while they wait. The band at the bottom holds processes that are asleep or waiting for the disk.

What the Koopman lens shows

The machine's state every 10 ms (how many cores are busy, how many processes are queued, and how much CPU each kind of job gets) is advanced by one linear operator. Its eigenvalues show how quickly load patterns fade. Surprise is how badly the operator fitted on the first 8 s predicts the next 10 ms.

Lab trace: HP Pro Tower 280 G9, 28 logical CPUs, Linux 7.0, captured with perf. Other traces: Linux 6.18 virtual machine with 2 cores, captured with tracefs, each repeated three times (one repetition shown).
